Years of contract administration, and the mess at an electrical business that proved we were right

Tradies Bureau is six years old now. It didn't start with one business's crisis. It came out of years of contract administration and project management work across builders, building designers and home energy assessors, alongside electricians. That's our heartland, and it's where the system was built and tested long before any one client pushed it to its limits.

One electrical business is where it got pushed hardest. It came to me after a split from its parent company, people disagreeing on strategy and direction, going their separate ways. I came on to manage their compliance, running it the way I'd always run it elsewhere: spreadsheets, trackers, diary entries.

That's where it got hard. Spreadsheets and diary entries work until you try to hand them to someone else, or step back yourself. Insurance lapsed. An audit landed. The owner's stress was palpable, and it didn't stay with him. The people around him got dragged into that vortex of negativity too.

I couldn't replicate myself with a spreadsheet. If I wanted to put myself out of a job, the tracking had to live somewhere that didn't depend on me remembering it.

The turning point was small. I took on subbie onboarding.

Suddenly the owner could stand on site and say to a tradie, "We're taking on new business. I love your work. Should we talk?" Then: "Email Naomi. She'll send over the agreements, you send them back to her." One sentence, and one person to hand it to. He stopped chasing paperwork and started talking about the future and what he was building.

Insurances and audits followed. I still got sucked in sometimes, and that's the honest bit. But that business is what proved the system had to exist properly, not just live in my own head and my own spreadsheets.

That's still the test today, across every trade we work with: does it take weight off an owner, or is it one more bloody portal to log into?
