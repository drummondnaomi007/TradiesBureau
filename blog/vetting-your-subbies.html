Who you put on site is who the client blames: vetting your subbies

When a job goes wrong because of a subbie, the first thing most owners say is that it was the subbie.

The client doesn't see it that way. They hired you. They expect the business they engaged to deliver the work, and that's the business they'll hold to it.

It bites hardest once you've grown. At the start you could check every ticket and every policy yourself. Now you're handing over really good jobs and you haven't got the hours to check everyone, but you still have to trust they've got the right accreditations and tickets. If a sub wasn't vetted or trained properly, your whole business is hanging off that one decision.

The flip side is good news. On a recent project we hired a builder, and the best feedback that came back from our client was about his subbies. He had the best subbies, and his whole team was fantastic.

Clients know how this works now. A builder isn't doing everything. There's an electrician, a plumber, a plasterer, a painter, and the client is judging all of them, not just the bloke who signed the contract. Whoever you put on site is your reputation, for better or worse.
