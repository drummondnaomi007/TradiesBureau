Getting started

Set up your business and see what applies to you.

Set up your business

  1. Go to check and answer the Compliance Checker's questions about your trades and the work you do.
  2. See everything that applies to you, then create your account.
  3. Open the link we email you to confirm your address. Your answers are saved with it.
  4. Open compliance/map to see what's on file and what's missing.

Run more than one business, or help look after someone else's? Sign in once and use Switch business to move between them.

Then add the rest

From there you can add compliance items, employees and subcontractors. Everything you add feeds the same weekly Sunday digest.

Adding a compliance item

Licences, insurance, subscriptions and vehicles, with their colours explained.

  1. Go to Compliance from the header.
  2. Choose a type: License/Cert, Insurance, Subscription, or Vehicle/Equipment.
  3. Enter an expiry or renewal date.
  4. Save. The item appears straight away on the Compliance page, and is in next Sunday's digest once the nightly job runs.

What the colours mean

The colour is worked out live from the date:

  • Red: expired, or due within 6 days.
  • Amber: due in 7 to 29 days.
  • Green: 30 days or more away.

Insurance

For insurance, also enter the cover amount and the insured name from the certificate of currency. The dashboard checks your policies against what your trade has to hold:

  • Electricians and solar businesses: $5m public liability in the REC's name.
  • Licensed plumbers and roofers: $5m public and products liability, plus warranty insurance of $50,000 per compliance certificate.
  • Building designers: $1.5m professional indemnity.
  • Anyone with staff: WorkCover.

Anything missing, expired or under the minimum shows under Insurance for your trade, and legal gaps go in the Sunday digest.

Jobs, certificates and SWMS

Track each job's certificates, SWMS and documents to sign.

Add a job

Add each job under Jobs: what it is, the customer, the address and the start date. Tick the certificates it needs.

  • Electricians get the Certificate of Electrical Safety ticked for them.
  • Plumbers, roofers and refrigeration techs get the plumbing compliance certificate on jobs of $750 or more.

Certificate deadlines

Certificate deadlines start when you mark the job complete. Enter each date as it's done, and the deadline clears.

  • Plumbing compliance certificate: to the customer and lodged with the BPC (through VBA360) within 5 days.
  • Certificate of Electrical Safety: to the customer within 1 month.
  • Prescribed electrical work: the inspection within 8 business days, the certificate within 4 business days of the inspection, and the customer's copy within 16 days of finishing.

High risk work and SWMS

If a job involves high risk construction work, like a fall of more than 2 metres or work near live electrical services, tick it on the job. A safe work method statement (SWMS) must be prepared before that work starts, so the job shows it as due on the start date until you record it.

Anything due shows on the dashboard straight away, and in the Sunday digest and WhatsApp alerts.

Getting documents signed

To get a quote, design agreement or terms of trade signed, use Send for signature on the job.

  1. Choose the PDF, say who signs it and their email, and add a note if you like. For an agreement both sides sign, tick Sign it myself first.
  2. They're emailed a link that works for 30 days, with the PDF attached.
  3. On the link they read the document, tick to agree and type their name, or decline with a reason, which you're emailed.
  4. Once they sign, you both get the signed copy by email. It has a signature page on the end showing who signed, when, the exact wording each person agreed to, and a fingerprint of the file.

If the file changes after it's sent, it can't be signed. From the job you can send the link again, copy it to send yourself, or withdraw it.

Waterproofing certificates

Waterproofers get a waterproofing certificate on each job:

  • Tick what was waterproofed: internal wet areas are done to AS 3740; balconies, roofs and podiums to AS 4654.2.
  • Note the rooms and the membrane system.
  • Record the dates: membrane applied, flood test or inspection before it's covered, certificate signed, and certificate given to the builder or owner.
  • Upload the signed certificate or photos.

None of these steps has a legal deadline, so the app doesn't invent one. Once the job is marked complete, a missing flood test or handover goes in the Sunday digest under needing your attention.

Keep the record for at least 10 years from completion: defect claims can come that late.

Employees and Fair Work

Fair Work statements, fixed-term contracts, and award and pay checks.

Fair Work statements

Upload the current Fair Work statements once, under Employees:

  • Fair Work Information Statement: every new employee.
  • Casual Employment Information Statement: casuals, and again after 12 months.
  • Fixed Term Contract Information Statement: fixed-term employees, before they start or as soon as possible after.

Each employee is sent the ones that apply to them and asked to confirm they got them, and anyone who hasn't is chased.

Fixed-term employees

For a fixed-term employee, record when the contract ends. Fair Work doesn't allow a fixed-term contract longer than 2 years, renewals included, or more than one renewal, unless an exception applies (an apprentice or trainee, pay above the high income threshold, or specialised skills among them).

The app warns if the dates run past 2 years, and the digest reminds you 30 days before a contract ends.

Award and pay

On each employee's page, Award and pay records which award covers the work they actually do (not their job title) and their classification.

Award rates go up every 1 July after the Fair Work Commission's annual wage review. After each 1 July the digest lists anyone whose pay hasn't been checked against the new rates. Check it, then press I've checked their pay today.

The app doesn't run payroll. Pay slips (within 1 working day of paying) and time and wage records (kept 7 years) stay in your payroll system.

Sharing your licence and insurance

Send customers and builders one live link instead of PDFs.

When a customer or builder asks for your licence and insurance, send them a link instead of digging out PDFs.

Send a link

  1. Go to Dashboard, then Share my credentials.
  2. Tick what to include and say who it's for.
  3. Choose how long it works: 30 days, 90 days, until the job is finished, or until you withdraw it.
  4. Copy, email or text the link.

What they see

  • Your registration and its number, and your ABN.
  • Each certificate of currency with its cover and expiry date, and the PDFs to download.
  • Links to check your licence on the official register (the BPC for builders and plumbers, Energy Safe Victoria for electricians) and your ABN on ABN Lookup.

The page reads your records each time it's opened, so it never goes out of date: upload your renewed policy and every link you've sent shows the new one. A certificate that has run out shows as expired and can't be downloaded.

Only your business's own items are offered, never staff documents, and policy numbers show only their last 4 digits. Add your ABN under Settings, Business details, and each licence's number when you add or edit it on the Dashboard.

Links for a job or contract

Pick a job or contract and the link is filled in from it. It shows on that job's page under Credentials shared, with when it was sent, opened and downloaded: your record of what you gave them.

  • On a contract, the link can also show the project's own certificates, such as its home warranty certificate, including ones you add after sending it.
  • When you send a quote from a job with Send for signature, tick Include my registration and insurance to add a link to the same email.

Your always-on link

Your always-on link has no customer or job on it. Put it on your website or in your email signature, or print its QR code for the ute. Changing what it shows keeps the same address, so a printed QR code keeps working.

Under Links you've shared you can see who opened each link, and withdraw one so it stops working straight away.

Subcontractors

Onboarding, certificates, insurance requirements and the contractor check.

Add a subcontractor

Add a subcontractor from the Subcontractors page with their name, trade and mobile number.

If whoever signs the trade agreement isn't the day-to-day contact (an office admin who lines up certs, say, with a director who signs), set a separate Signatory name and email on the same form. Leave it blank and the subcontractor's own name and email are used for everything, including where the agreement is emailed once it's ready to sign.

Set up onboarding first

It's worth setting up the onboarding instructions and checklist once, before you add anyone, also from the Subcontractors page:

  • Upload a PDF: an induction pack, site rules, anything you'd otherwise send separately.
  • Add as many checklist items as you like, such as "Confirmed public liability cover with your insurer".
  • Both are business-wide, shown to every subcontractor whatever their trade.
  • An item can only be added or removed, not edited or reordered. Remove and re-add it to change the wording.
  • A subcontractor ticking an item is them confirming it themselves. It's for your own view of their onboarding and doesn't block anything on their link.

How onboarding works

Tradies Bureau makes a one-time onboarding link for the subcontractor's trade. They upload each required document with an expiry date (the app reads the date off the certificate where it can, and it can always be changed). Their status turns Compliant or Non-Compliant, or stays Pending, as certificates come in and later expire.

If you've uploaded a blank agreement template for that trade (on the Subcontractors page, one PDF per trade under Subcontractor agreement templates), it unlocks for the subcontractor to sign once every required certificate is on file. See Subcontractor agreement signing for how that works.

Insurance requirements

Insurance certificates ask the subcontractor for the cover amount. The law's minimum for their trade always applies, for example $5m public liability for electricians and plumbers, and warranty insurance of $50,000 per certificate for plumbers ($100,000 on commercial work).

To ask for more, go to Settings, Business, Subcontractor requirements:

  • Public liability and professional indemnity figures for any job, and higher ones for commercial or mixed-use contracts.
  • Professional indemnity from chosen trades (building designers need it by law anyway).
  • A WorkCover certificate of currency from every subcontractor. A sole trader with no employees confirms that instead, and again each year.

Subbies you've already invited get the new certificates on their list at the next recheck, which runs straight away when you save. A certificate under the figure shows red ("$5m cover, $10m required") and the subbie becomes non-compliant. The commercial figure is checked against each commercial or mixed-use contract they're engaged on.

Contractor or employee?

On each subcontractor's page, answer ten questions about how the arrangement really works, plus two about super:

  • Who decides how and when the work is done.
  • Whether they can send someone else, and whose tools are used.
  • How they're paid and who carries the risk.
  • Whether they work for others, and whether they're presented as part of your business.

Since August 2024 Fair Work judges the whole relationship, not just the contract or an ABN. If you're accused of sham contracting, you have to show you reasonably believed they were a contractor. Saving keeps a dated record of your answers.

Two or more signs of employment go in the digest, and it suggests checking again after a year. It's guidance, not legal advice.

Subcontractor agreement signing

How subbies sign their agreement online, and how to fix a mistake.

How they sign

Once every required certificate for a subcontractor's trade is uploaded, their onboarding link unlocks a "{Trade} Agreement" card with a link to read the template PDF.

  1. They type their full name.
  2. They tick "I have read the agreement above and I'm signing it electronically as my legally binding signature."
  3. They click Sign agreement. The signature is recorded straight away, timestamped and tied to the exact wording of the agreement at that moment.

It's a typed e-signature: there's no drawn signature pad and nothing to print, scan or upload back.

Know its limits

The onboarding link isn't tied to a person's identity. Whoever holds the link can type any name, and there's no check that it matches. The Signatory name and email you set control who the link and agreement are emailed to, not who's allowed to sign.

Treat it like a document posted to the right person's address: fine for a routine subcontractor agreement, but know its limits for anything higher-stakes.

Made a mistake?

Wrong name typed, wrong person signed, or the template has changed? Either of you can void the signature and start again:

  • You: Void signature & allow re-signing, on the subcontractor's detail page.
  • The subcontractor: Made a mistake? Void and re-sign, under the signed agreement on their onboarding link.

Both ask for a short reason, then permanently void that signature. The audit log keeps the original signature and the reason, so nothing is silently lost. There's no undo; signing again is the only way back.

Copies and the onboarding packet

Each subcontractor signs their own copy, kept against them and that version of the agreement. When they sign, they're emailed their copy with a signature page on the end showing who signed, when, the wording they agreed to, and a fingerprint of the document.

Download packet (.zip) on the subcontractor's page builds a complete onboarding packet whenever you need it:

  • A plain-text manifest.
  • Every uploaded certificate.
  • The agreement template.
  • A signature receipt: who signed, when, and which version they agreed to.

The same zip is emailed to you the moment a subcontractor finishes signing, so you don't have to remember to come back for it.

Turning on Contract Administration

Switch on the contract screens if you run formal contracts.

Turn on Contract Administration & Work Orders from Settings, Business if you run formal contracts: domestic building work, a head contract with a client, or subcontracting under someone else.

It's off by default: most trades only need the compliance calendar the app starts with.

Switching it on adds a Contract Admin menu (Projects, Contracts, Providers) and a Contract rules item under Settings. Switching it off hides all four again. Nothing already logged is deleted.

Projects, Contracts and Providers

How projects group contracts, and which rules each kind of contract follows.

Projects

A Project groups every contract on one physical job: the client contract, any subcontractors you've engaged on it, and (if you're the one being subcontracted) the contract with whoever engaged you. Add a Project from the Projects page, then add its contracts from the Project's own page.

Adding a contract

  1. Choose which way it runs: "I'm the head contractor" (you're contracting with a Client) or "I'm the subcontractor" (an external Provider engaged you). Pick an existing Client or Provider, or type a new one and it's created on the spot.
  2. Say what kind of work it is: work on a home, commercial, or mixed use (like a shop with a flat above), who the client is, and for homes, how many storeys.

That decides which rules apply. You can correct any of it later with Edit on the contract, and the rules re-check themselves.

Which rules apply

  • A client contract for work on a home is a domestic building contract: cooling-off; home warranty over $20,000 on buildings up to 3 storeys (or the old domestic building insurance over $16,000 for a contract signed before 1 July 2026); and no money taken until the policy is in place.
  • Commercial work is a head contract.
  • Security of Payment covers your claims, except on a domestic contract with a home owner, landlord or owners corporation. A developer who builds homes as a business is covered.
  • For plumbers, commercial or mixed-use jobs need warranty insurance of $100,000 per certificate, not $50,000.

Design and other services

A client contract can be for design or other professional services instead of building work. Design by an architect, a registered building designer or an endorsed building engineer is outside the Domestic Building Contracts Act. A services agreement has none of its rules (cooling-off, home warranty, deposit and stage payment limits) and no permit, inspections, practical completion or handover sections. You can still send the agreement to sign, and record variations and payment claims.

Security of Payment covers design services on commercial work and for developers. Whether it covers a design claim on a home owner isn't confirmed, so the app sets no payment deadlines there.

Contracts and Providers pages

  • Contracts (in the Contract Admin menu) lists every contract across every project, each linking to its own page and project. Handy for a quick scan.
  • Providers lists every external head contractor you've subcontracted under. Open one to see every contract they've engaged you on.

Working a contract day-to-day

Work orders, variations, claims, RFIs, inspections and completion.

Open a contract from its project, or from the Contracts list, to reach its action screens.

Work orders

Work orders break the job into stages, each with a checklist of deliverables you tick off as the work progresses.

Variations, payment claims and RFIs

All three follow the same two steps: log the request, then record what happens next as a separate action.

  • Variation: the builder's signature must be recorded before the client's approval window starts.
  • Payment claim: the 10-business-day schedule deadline and the payment deadline start the moment you log it.
  • RFI: raised by or to the client, a subcontractor on the job, an upstream provider, or a named third party such as a building surveyor. Pick from a list for that contract, or type a name. It's answered once a response comes back.

Mandatory inspections

Inspections follow the stages on the building permit.

  1. Under Inspections on the contract, tick the stages the permit lists: before placing a footing, before pouring reinforced concrete, completion of framework, any other stage it names, and final. Concrete and other stages can be listed once per member.
  2. As each stage is reached, record it and when you told the building surveyor.
  3. Record the inspection booking, then whether it passed (with the inspector) or didn't (with what needs fixing, after which you notify the surveyor again).

The law says to tell the surveyor without delay, so a stage reached without a notice goes straight into the Sunday digest. A stage the permit doesn't list can be marked not in the permit.

RFIs and inspections both take photo and file attachments once raised.

Your client's portal

If the contract has a client, use Invite client to portal to send them a link. They can see progress and record their own variation and payment approvals, so you don't enter them on their behalf.

Subcontractors on the job

Log your own subcontractors under Subcontractors engaged: pick who and what they're doing. Their licence and insurance status comes straight from the certificates their onboarding already tracks, so nothing is entered twice. If one of their certificates lapses, the engagement shows it without you checking their profile.

Practical completion and defects

When the job reaches practical completion, record it on the Practical completion card: the date, who certified it (usually the building surveyor), and an optional certificate reference.

  • It's a one-time, one-way action: the contract's practical completion date and status are set together.
  • A final payment claim can only be raised after it's recorded; one raised before is refused.
  • From that date the defects liability period runs. Log a defect with a description and an optional due date on the Defects card, then resolve it as Rectified (with the date) or Waived.

What Contract Administration delivers

Payment deadlines, domestic contract checks, delays, handover and the audit trail.

This isn't a project schedule or an accounting package: there's no Gantt chart, budget-to-complete or BAS. It's a contract-administration log built around Victorian construction law (the Domestic Building Contracts Act, the Security of Payment Act and the Building Regulations 2018), so the paperwork a dispute would ask for (who agreed to what, and when) is a proper record instead of an email thread.

Payment claims and Security of Payment

Log a claim and both Security of Payment Act deadlines are set straight away, counted in real Victorian business days (weekends, public holidays and the 22 December to 10 January shutdown left out):

  • 10 business days for the other party to give a payment schedule.
  • Separately, counted from the claim date: 10 business days to pay by default, or your contract's own payment term (never more than 20 business days, the Act's ceiling).

Miss the 10-day schedule window and the full claimed amount becomes payable, whether or not it was agreed. A daily check catches that automatically.

  • These deadlines apply to head contracts and subcontracts, and to domestic contracts with a developer who builds homes as a business.
  • The Act doesn't cover a domestic contract with a home owner, landlord or owners corporation, so those claims follow the contract's own terms with no SOPA deadlines.

Treat these as a strong starting point, not legal advice, until a construction lawyer confirms them.

Claims to the owner on a domestic contract

Each claim picks its stage (deposit, base, frame, lock-up, fixing, final, or another progress payment) and is checked against the Domestic Building Contracts Act before it's logged:

  • Deposit: at most 10% of a price under $20,000, or 5% from $20,000.
  • A new home built through all stages (set under Edit on the contract): base 10%, frame 15%, lock-up 35% and fixing 25% of the price, counting agreed variations.
  • Frame waits until the frame inspection is recorded as completed.
  • Final waits for practical completion, and for the owner to have the occupancy permit or certificate of final inspection, recorded in its own section.

A claim the Act doesn't allow is refused with the reason. For renovations and builds that stop earlier, the app reminds you that each payment must match the progress of the work and leaves the figures to you.

Delays and extensions of time

  1. First enter your contract's time terms with Edit terms: the notice period for delays (contracts differ, so the app never assumes one) and the completion date or building period.
  2. Log every delay under Delays and extensions of time: what caused it, when it started, when you became aware of it, and when it ended.
  3. Each delay gets a notice deadline, shown on the dashboard and in the digest until you record the notice you gave and the days claimed.
  4. Record the other party's answer (granted, partly granted or rejected). The revised completion date moves by the days granted.

Tick "pause this contract's pausable deadlines" when logging a delay to pause them while it lasts. With no notice period entered, a delay with no notice shows in the digest as needing attention.

Handover

The Handover section on the contract is the checklist of what the owner or client gets from you (on a subcontract, what the builder gets). Set up checklist suggests items from the contract:

  • A compliance certificate from each engaged subbie whose trade needs one: plumbing for work of $750 or more, an electrical safety certificate for electrical work, waterproofing.
  • The home warranty certificate, where it applies.
  • As-built drawings, product warranties and manuals, and keys.
  • Anything else the building surveyor or contract asks for, such as glazing, termite barrier or insulation certificates, or a building manual for a new Class 1b, 2 or 3 building from 1 February 2027.

Mark each item received when a subbie sends it, and handed over when it goes to the owner (or not needed, with a reason), and attach the file. The occupancy permit or certificate of final inspection comes first, from its own section. Once everything is handed over, the handover obligation is done.

No law sets a date for handover, so the app doesn't invent one. Once practical completion or the occupancy permit is recorded, anything outstanding goes in the Sunday digest as needing your attention.

Minimum financial requirements

Domestic builders are held to the minimum financial requirements from 1 July 2026. Under Settings, Business, enter the Maximum Construction Capacity the BPC approved, and your adjusted net tangible assets with their date.

  • The app adds up your signed domestic contracts that haven't reached practical completion (contract price including agreed variations) and shows how much of your capacity they use.
  • An unsigned domestic contract that would take you over shows a warning, because a new contract can't take you over.
  • Being over, assets under 5% of the capacity, or domestic work under way with no capacity entered goes in the Sunday digest.
  • The settings page also lists the duties there's nothing to count for: quarterly management accounts, given to the BPC within 14 business days of a written request, and telling the BPC within 5 business days if you can't pay your debts.

The contract threshold

The threshold for a major domestic building contract (cooling-off, deposit and progress-payment limits, and registration) is $10,000 today. It rises to $20,000 when the Building and Plumbing Administration and Enforcement Act 2026 starts, which must be by 1 December 2027 at the latest. The app switches over by itself then, going by each contract's signing date. If an earlier start date is proclaimed, it's one setting to change.

Claims from your own subcontractors

Claims your subcontractors serve on you run the other way, and the deadlines are yours. Log each one under Claims from subcontractors on the contract, with the date you received it.

  • Payment schedule: within 10 business days (sooner if the subcontract says so), saying how much you'll pay, with reasons if it's less than the claim.
  • Payment: due 10 business days after the claim, or by the subcontract's own term, never more than 20.
  • No schedule in time means the whole claim is owed, and a late schedule doesn't change that.
  • Subcontracts are covered on every job, including ones for home owners.

Each unpaid claim shows on the dashboard and in the Sunday digest: first the schedule, then the payment, with the amount owed.

Variations

Variations are two separate steps: the builder signs before the client's approval window opens. That order is enforced by which dates can be recorded, the same shape a "the client says they never approved that" dispute would ask you to prove.

  • When you sign a variation, it's emailed to the client to approve, with a PDF and a link to their portal that works for 30 days (their current link is reused if it has more than a week left).
  • The contract shows when it was emailed, and Send to the client again sends it once more.
  • If it can't be emailed (no email address, or email isn't set up), you're given the link to send yourself.
  • Once they approve, you both get the PDF with both signatures, and either of you can download it any time.

The audit trail

Every action in Contract Administration (signing a variation, answering an RFI, marking a claim paid) writes an audit-log entry with who, when, and the actual before and after values. That's the record a dispute would ask for, kept as a side effect of normal use.

An honest limit

The full 18-rule checklist doesn't yet appear as one dashboard of every obligation on a job. Most rules have their own tracking, all described above: payment claims, the two-step variation sign-off, RFIs, mandatory inspections, subcontractor compliance, practical completion, the defects liability period, delays and handover.

The rest (the cooling-off period, builder registration and insurance checks, home warranty insurance, building permit issue, the limitation tracker, and the dispute and conciliation step) are in the rule catalogue with their legal citations and show in the contract's obligations list, but don't have a screen of their own yet.

Every rule ships marked Unverified until a construction lawyer checks it. Treat the built-in dates as a strong starting point, not legal advice.

Reading your Sunday digest

What's in the weekly email and how your score is worked out.

Every Sunday at 6:30am you get one email, grouped into three parts, plus your overall compliance score:

  • Needs action now.
  • Coming up.
  • Up to date.

Your compliance score

Score = (green items × 1 + amber items × 0.5) ÷ total items × 100.

Needs your attention

Things with no date to count down to that shouldn't wait, for example:

  • An inspection stage reached without telling the building surveyor.
  • Handover still outstanding after completion.
  • Domestic work over your building capacity.
  • Apprentice supervision over the ratios.

Apprentice supervision

Energy Safe's requirements for electrical apprentices have been mandatory since 1 September 2025. From each apprenticeship's year and supervising electrician, the app flags:

  • A supervisor with more than one first-year apprentice (first-years need direct supervision, one to one).
  • A supervisor with more than 5 apprentices in all.

Both show on the apprentice's and the supervisor's employee pages too. The ratios for second and third years under general supervision aren't checked until they're confirmed.

For employees: ticking off your onboarding.

Your employer sent you a link that opens straight to your onboarding checklist. No account or password needed.

  1. Open the link.
  2. Tick off each item as you finish it: safety induction, PPE issued, compliance documents collected, super fund set up.

Your employer sees your progress as you go.

Uploading your certs

For subcontractors: uploading certificates and signing your agreement.

Follow the link the business sent you. No account or password needed.

Before you start

If the business has set up onboarding instructions, you'll see a link to read them first, and maybe a short checklist of things to confirm on your side (like your insurance cover). Ticking those is just for your record and doesn't block anything.

Upload your certificates

  1. Upload each required document and type in its expiry date.
  2. For insurance, also type in the cover amount from the certificate of currency. Where the law sets a minimum for your trade (for example $5m public liability for electricians and plumbers), cover under it doesn't count.
  3. If your trade has optional work (like gas fitting), tick it to show the extra certificate it needs.

Asked for a WorkCover certificate but you don't employ anyone? Choose "I don't employ anyone" under it, tick to confirm and type your name. You'll be asked to confirm again after a year.

Sign your agreement

Once every required certificate is uploaded, any trade agreement the business wants you to sign unlocks on the same page.

  1. Read the agreement.
  2. Type your full name.
  3. Tick the box confirming you're signing electronically, and submit. That's your legally binding signature: nothing to print, scan or post back.

Made a mistake?

Typed the wrong name, or someone else should have signed? Use Made a mistake? Void and re-sign under the signed agreement, then sign again. No need to contact the business.

FAQ

Quick answers about sign-in, contracts, signatures and storage.

Getting in

Do I need a password?

No. You sign in with a link sent to your email, and it works for 7 days.

What happens if my certificate expires?

You, and whoever hired you, see it turn red in the next update, and critical alerts are sent straight away.

Contract Administration

What's Contract Administration & Work Orders?

An optional set of screens (Projects, Contracts and Providers in their own Contract Admin menu, plus Contract rules under Settings) for businesses running formal domestic building contracts, subcontracting, or head-contract work. It's off by default, since most trades never need it, and switching it on or off from Settings, Business never deletes anything already logged.

What's the difference between a Provider and a Client?

A Client is who you're contracting with as head contractor. A Provider is the reverse: an external head contractor who's engaged you as their subcontractor. A contract has one or the other, never both.

Can a client see the rest of my business through their portal link?

No. It only ever shows their own contract or contracts with you, never other clients, other projects, or anything else in your Tradies Bureau.

Are the Contract Administration rules legally verified?

Not yet. Every rule ships marked Unverified until a construction lawyer checks it, and can be edited any time at Settings, Contract rules. Treat the built-in due dates as a strong starting point, not legal advice, until they're signed off.

Subcontractor agreements

Where do I upload a subcontractor's agreement template?

On the Subcontractors page, under Subcontractor agreement templates: one blank PDF per trade. It only unlocks for a subcontractor to sign once every required certificate for their trade is on file.

Is a typed e-signature legally binding?

It's a genuine, timestamped, auditable electronic signature, the same mechanism used for a variation sign-off or a payment-claim approval. It isn't identity-checked, though: anyone holding the onboarding link can type any name. Use it for routine subcontractor agreements, and the Signatory field and your own judgement for anything higher-stakes.

A subcontractor signed with the wrong name, or the wrong person signed. Now what?

Void it (either of you can, from their onboarding link or their page in the app), give a short reason, and they can sign again from scratch. The original signature and the reason both stay in the audit log.

Files

Where do uploaded photos and documents go?

Google Drive, through whichever account is connected under Settings, Business. A project can have its own folder; everything else uses the business-wide one. See Document storage (Google Drive).

What happens if Google Drive isn't connected?

Uploads still save. You just won't get a document link, and you'll see a message pointing to the health report. Nothing is silently lost.
