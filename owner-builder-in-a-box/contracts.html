Subbing out work

You're the builder now. Contract like one.

As an owner-builder you engage each trade directly. Good contracts are how you control price, quality and time, and how you protect yourself when something goes wrong.

3. What the law says

Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995

  • Major domestic building contract (over $10,000*): must be in writing, with a registered builder, and include the prescribed information.
  • Deposit caps: 10% if the contract price is under $20,000, and 5% if it's $20,000 or more.
  • Home warranty cover: for contracts over $20,000 signed from 1 July 2026, the builder arranges cover under the First Resort Home Warranty Scheme.
  • Statutory warranties (s8) apply to domestic building work: good workmanship, suitable materials, compliance with the law and the plans, and fitness for purpose.
  • If one builder does several stages, progress payment caps apply (e.g. base, frame, lock-up, fixing).

Other laws that touch your trades

  • Building Act 1993: registration of building practitioners, and plumbing licensing through the BPC.
  • Electricity Safety Act 1998: licensed electricians, Certificates of Electrical Safety.
  • OHS Act 2004 & Regulations 2017: SWMS, site safety, principal contractor.
  • Security of Payment Act 2002: generally doesn't apply to domestic building work for an owner who lives in, or will live in, the home. Pay fairly anyway, because reputation travels.
  • Australian Consumer Law: guarantees on goods and services, plus unfair contract terms rules.

5. Must-have clauses in every trade contract

Who, what & how much

  • Parties, ABN, licence / registration numbers
  • Scope tied to drawing and spec revision numbers
  • Inclusions and exclusions
  • Contract price, GST, and what's fixed vs provisional
  • Provisional sums and prime cost items, with allowances stated

When & how

  • Start date, duration, site access & working hours
  • Who supplies what (materials, bins, scaffold, power)
  • Site rules: induction, SWMS, cleaning, waste, toilet
  • Hold points: work isn't covered until it's inspected
  • Delays & extensions of time process

Money

  • Deposit (within statutory caps) and milestone payments
  • Payment only for completed, inspected work
  • Tax invoice requirements and payment terms (e.g. 7–14 days)
  • Retention (e.g. 5%, released after the defects period)
  • Final payment conditional on compliance certificates

When things change or go wrong

  • Variations in writing, priced, signed before the work
  • Defects liability period (e.g. 3–12 months) and response times
  • Statutory warranties acknowledged
  • Insurance obligations and proof of cover
  • Dispute resolution steps and termination rights

6. Paying trades

Golden rule: never pay ahead of the work.

Pay for work that's complete, inspected (where required) and matches the contract. Big upfront payments are the number-one way owner-builders lose money.

  1. ClaimTrade submits a progress claim or tax invoice referencing the contract milestone.
  2. CheckWalk the work. Compare it with the scope and photos. Confirm the inspection passed.
  3. ApproveApprove in full or in part. Record any retention held.
  4. PayPay within the contract terms. Log it in the payment register.
  5. ReconcileUpdate Budget → Committed → Paid → Forecast.

8. Defects & disputes

  1. DocumentDated photos, the contract clause and a description of the defect.
  2. Notify in writingGive a reasonable time to fix (e.g. 10 business days).
  3. Meet on siteMost disputes are solved in a 20-minute walk-through.
  4. BPC dispute resolutionDomestic building disputes now go through the Building and Plumbing Commission (formerly DBDRV).
  5. VCATIf it's still unresolved, the Building and Property List.

9. Trade agreement outline

The structure the app's template generator uses. Have a lawyer review your final version. For major domestic building contracts, use an industry-standard form (e.g. HIA, MBAV or Consumer Affairs Victoria guidance).

Owner-builder trade agreement: section outline
  1. Parties, property address, building permit & certificate of consent numbers
  2. Contract documents & order of precedence (drawings rev, spec rev, quote, this agreement)
  3. Scope of works, inclusions, exclusions
  4. Contract price, GST, provisional sums, prime cost items
  5. Program: start, duration, hold points, extensions of time
  6. Site access, supply responsibilities, site rules & OHS (SWMS, induction)
  7. Insurance & licensing warranties, with certificates attached
  8. Payments: deposit, milestones, retention, invoice requirements
  9. Variations procedure
  10. Compliance certificates & documents due before final payment
  11. Defects liability period, statutory warranties
  12. Dispute resolution, termination, notices
  13. Signatures & date
