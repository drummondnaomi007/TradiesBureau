Owner-builder Q&A · Victoria

Every question, answered in plain English.

The questions owner-builders actually ask, grouped by topic and searchable. Start typing below.

Getting started

What is an owner-builder?

An owner-builder is a landowner who takes legal responsibility for domestic building work on their own land, instead of engaging a registered builder to manage the whole job. You take on the builder's responsibilities: quality, compliance, safety, and coordinating and paying trades. You can do some work yourself and hire trades for the rest.

Do I have to do the building work myself?

No. Most owner-builders act as project manager and engage trades for most of the work. You can do the non-licensed work yourself if you're capable, such as some carpentry, painting or landscaping. Plumbing, gasfitting and electrical work must always be done by licensed practitioners.

How much can I really save?

A builder's margin and overheads are commonly 15–25% of the build cost. Realistic owner-builder savings are often 10–20% after allowing for your time, mistakes, higher finance costs and missed trade discounts. Savings come from managing well, not just from cutting out the builder.

How long does an owner-build take?

Plan on 18–24 months from idea to occupancy for a typical new home. That's roughly 6–11 months of design, approvals and procurement, then 9–13 months on site. Owner-builders working part-time usually take longer. See the gateways program.

What skills do I need?

Organisation matters more than trade skills. You'll be reading plans, scheduling, negotiating, checking quality, managing money and paperwork, and keeping people safe. If you run projects or a household budget well, you're most of the way there. Owner Builder in a Box fills in the rest.

Eligibility & certificate of consent

When do I need a certificate of consent?

When the value of the domestic building work is over $20,000 (it was $16,000 before 1 July 2026). Value means labour plus materials plus GST, including a market value for your own labour. Below that you can owner-build without consent, but you still need any building permit the work requires.

Who is eligible?
  • You own the land, or you're a director of the owning company or a beneficiary of the owning trust
  • The work is for a single dwelling (or ancillary to it)
  • You live in it, or intend to (not required for a small second dwelling)
  • You're not in the business of building
  • No owner-builder permit in the past 5 years (with exceptions)
  • You've passed the BPC eLearning assessment and done construction induction training

See the eligibility checklist.

What is the eLearning assessment?

A free online course and assessment run by the Building and Plumbing Commission. It covers your legal responsibilities, safety, contracts and the building process, and takes about 2–3 hours. You need a pass before applying for consent.

Can I owner-build again within five years?

Generally not on a different property. The exceptions are further work on the same home, building a small second dwelling, or an exemption granted by the BPC for special circumstances such as a substantial change in circumstances or hardship.

Can I owner-build if my partner and I own the land together?

Yes. Each owner-builder named needs to meet the requirements. The five-year rule also looks at people you co-own, or have co-owned, property with, so check both your histories.

Can I owner-build a house to sell or rent out?

Owner-builder consent is for a home you live in or intend to live in, apart from a small second dwelling. Building to sell or rent can put you "in the business of building", which requires registration.

Who regulates owner-builders now: the VBA or the BPC?

The Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC). It replaced the Victorian Building Authority, and from 1 July 2026 it also absorbed domestic building insurance (formerly VMIA) and dispute resolution (formerly DBDRV). Older guides and forms may still say VBA.

Permits & inspections

Do I need a planning permit and a building permit?

You always need a building permit for a new home. You need a planning permit only if your zone or overlays require one, for example heritage, bushfire, flood, vegetation or some neighbourhood character controls. Check your Planning Property Report on VicPlan, and ask council if you're unsure.

Who issues the building permit?

A relevant building surveyor (RBS) that you appoint. It can be a private surveyor or the council's. They assess your plans, issue the permit, carry out the mandatory inspections and issue the occupancy permit or certificate of final inspection.

What inspections are mandatory?

Usually before pouring footings, before pouring the slab, at frame stage and at final. Your permit may add more, such as a pre-plaster or a retaining wall inspection. You must notify the RBS at each stage and not cover work before it's inspected.

What documents does the surveyor need at the end?

Typically: plumbing compliance certificates, Certificate of Electrical Safety, waterproofing, termite and glazing certificates, insulation and energy compliance, smoke alarm certificate, and engineer sign-offs where required. Collect them as each trade finishes and don't release final payment until you have them.

What is an occupancy permit and why does it matter?

It's the RBS confirming a new home is suitable to live in. It's an offence to occupy a new dwelling without one, and your lender and insurer will ask for it.

What does the 7-star energy rating mean for me?

New Victorian homes must meet NCC 2022 energy efficiency: a 7-star NatHERS thermal rating plus a whole-of-home energy budget covering appliances, hot water and solar. Your energy assessor's report sets the insulation, glazing and fixed appliances, so buy exactly what it says.

Can I change the design during construction?

Minor non-structural changes are usually fine. Anything affecting structure, siting, fire safety or energy compliance needs an amended permit from your RBS before you build it.

Trades & contracts

Do my trades need to be registered?

Plumbers and gasfitters must be BPC licensed or registered, and electricians must be licensed by Energy Safe Victoria, regardless of job size. For other domestic building work over $10,000, the trade generally needs to be a registered domestic builder with a written major domestic building contract, with some single-trade exemptions. An increase in that threshold to $20,000 has been proposed.

How many quotes should I get?

Three is the minimum per package. Five for big packages like slab, frame and roof. Send every tenderer the same RFQ pack so the prices are comparable, then level and score them with the tender tool.

Should I always pick the cheapest quote?

No. Level the quotes first by adding the cost of exclusions and normalising provisional sums. Then weigh price against experience, availability, quality and communication. A quote well below the others usually means something is missing from the scope.

How much deposit can a trade ask for?

Under a major domestic building contract: no more than 10% if the contract price is under $20,000, and 5% if it's $20,000 or more. For smaller trade jobs, keep deposits minimal. Pay for completed, inspected work, not in advance.

What should be in a trade contract?

Scope tied to drawing revisions, inclusions and exclusions, price and GST, program, payment milestones, variations in writing, defects period, insurance, SWMS and site rules, and compliance certificates required before final payment. See the must-have clauses.

What if a trade does defective work?

Document it with photos, notify them in writing with a reasonable time to fix, and meet on site. If it's unresolved, use the BPC's domestic building dispute resolution, then VCAT. Statutory warranties under the Domestic Building Contracts Act protect you.

Can I hire workers directly and pay them wages?

You can, but then you're an employer, with obligations for WorkCover insurance, superannuation, PAYG, awards and OHS. Most owner-builders engage trades as contractors with their own ABN and insurance.

Money & finance

Will a bank lend to an owner-builder?

Some will, often with a lower maximum loan-to-value ratio, a detailed costing, fixed-price quotes, and progress draws released only after a valuer inspects each stage. Talk to a broker who knows construction lending before you buy land or commit to a design.

How much contingency should I allow?

At least 10%, and 15% for sloping, rocky or bushfire-prone sites or renovations. Contingency is for risks, not upgrades. Track it separately in your budget.

Can I get the First Home Owner Grant?

Owner-builders building their first home may be eligible, subject to value caps, residence requirements and timing rules such as when the foundations are laid. Check the current rules with the State Revenue Office before you start.

Do I pay or claim GST?

If you're building your own home to live in, you're generally not carrying on an enterprise, so you pay GST on trade invoices but can't claim it back. Building to sell can change that, so get tax advice.

How do I stop the budget blowing out?

Freeze scope at gateway G1. Get fixed prices wherever you can. Use written variations. Track Budget → Committed → Paid → Forecast every week. And never pay ahead of work.

Safety & insurance

Am I responsible for safety on site?

Yes. As the person managing or controlling the site you have duties under the OHS Act 2004. For projects of $350,000 or more you're the principal contractor unless you appoint one. That means a safety coordination plan, site induction, SWMS management and signage.

What is a SWMS?

A Safe Work Method Statement is required before any high-risk construction work, such as work at heights over 2 m, trenches over 1.5 m, structural alterations or work near powerlines. The trade prepares it. You check it's on site and being followed.

What insurance do I need?

Construction (contract works) insurance and public liability are strongly recommended, and lenders usually require them. Also consider personal accident cover. Check every trade's public liability and WorkCover certificates of currency, and track their expiry dates.

Does everyone on site need a white card?

Yes. Anyone doing construction work needs construction induction training, and so do you. It's also part of the owner-builder eligibility criteria.

Selling & after completion

What if I sell within 6.5 years?

Under s137B of the Building Act you must, before signing the contract of sale: (1) get a defects report from a prescribed building practitioner, no more than 6 months old, on the BPC template; and (2) for work over $20,000, comply with the statutory insurance scheme by buying owner-builder cover. The buyer gets copies. Selling without them is an offence, and the buyer may be able to end the contract.

What changed for owner-builder insurance on 1 July 2026?

The old last-resort domestic building insurance was replaced by the First Resort Home Warranty Scheme, run by the BPC. Buyers can claim without the owner-builder having died, disappeared or become insolvent. The trigger threshold rose to $20,000.

What records should I keep, and for how long?

Keep everything for at least 10 years: permits, stamped plans, certificates, contracts, invoices, warranties, photos and the site diary. A complete handover file makes selling, insurance claims and future renovations much easier. My Build keeps it all in one place.

Am I liable for defects after I finish?

If you sell, the buyer can rely on statutory warranties for owner-builder work for a period, and the insurance scheme backs claims. In practice that's why the defects report and insurance exist. Build it right, document it, and fix issues before sale.
