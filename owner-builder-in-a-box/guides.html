Buying guides & specs

Know what to ask for before you're at the counter.

Stage-by-stage guides to what you're buying, the Australian Standards that apply, the spec to write down and the questions to ask suppliers. Save your choices in My Build.

Base stage

Concrete footings & slab

Standards: AS 2870 (residential slabs & footings), AS 3600 (concrete structures).

  • Spec to confirm with your engineer: slab type (waffle or raft), concrete strength (typically N20–N32 MPa), slump, mesh (e.g. SL82), bar sizes, edge beam depths, cover to steel.
  • Vapour barrier: 0.2 mm polyethylene, lapped and taped. Check the energy report for slab-edge insulation.
  • Termite management to AS 3660.1: physical barrier or chemical. Get the certificate and durable notice.

Ask the supplier / concreter: Is pump hire included? What's the allowance for over-dig and extra concrete? Who books the pre-pour inspection? Will you give me delivery dockets showing MPa?

Site drainage & stormwater
  • Pipe class and diameter per the engineer's drainage plan (commonly 90 mm and 100 mm PVC).
  • Rainwater tank size if your energy or water compliance relies on it (often 2,000 L or more connected to toilets or laundry).
  • Connection to the legal point of discharge. Your plumber lodges a compliance certificate.

Frame & roof

Wall frames & roof trusses

Standards: AS 1684 (timber framing), AS 4440 (nail-plated trusses), NASH standard for light steel.

  • Timber grade: MGP10 / MGP12 pine is standard. Use H2 treated in termite zones, or light-gauge steel.
  • Wind classification (e.g. N2, N3) from your engineer. It drives tie-downs and bracing.
  • Get the frame & truss layout, design certificate and bracing plan with the delivery.

Ask: Is it pre-cut or prefabricated? Are lintels, beams and bracing included? What's the lead time? Do you set out the trusses on site?

Roofing: metal or tiles

Standards: AS 1562.1 (metal roofing), AS 2050 (tiles), AS 3959 (bushfire).

  • Metal: 0.42 mm BMT is standard. Choose the colour's solar absorptance to match the energy report.
  • Anticon or sarking blanket for thermal and condensation control (NCC 2022 condensation rules).
  • Gutter overflow provisions and downpipe spacing per AS/NZS 3500.3.
  • BAL-rated ember guards and screens if you're in a bushfire prone area.

Lock-up

Windows & external doors

Standards: AS 2047 (windows), AS 1288 (glass selection), AS 3959 (BAL).

  • Use the U-value and SHGC in your energy report. They are part of your 7-star compliance and must be matched.
  • Double glazing in thermally broken or uPVC frames is typical for 7-star compliance in Victoria.
  • Wind rating to match the site's wind class. BAL rating if required.
  • Keep the WERS label and AS 2047 certificate for your building surveyor.

Ask: Do these U-value and SHGC figures match my energy report? What's the lead time? Are flashings and reveals included? Is there a warranty on hardware and seals?

Wall wrap, cladding & bricks
  • Vapour-permeable wall wrap (NCC 2022 condensation provisions). Check its class against the specification.
  • Bricks: order about 5% extra from the same batch to avoid colour variation. Check wall ties and weep holes.
  • Fibre cement, timber or metal cladding: fix it to the manufacturer's installation guide. Warranties depend on it.

Services

Insulation

Standard: AS/NZS 4859.1. Values come from your energy report.

  • Typical Victorian 7-star: ceiling around R6.0, external walls around R2.5, plus underfloor if the floor is suspended.
  • Photograph every wall before plaster. The building surveyor may ask for an installation certificate.
Hot water, heating & all-electric homes
  • New Victorian homes that need a planning permit (applied for from 1 January 2024) can't connect to reticulated gas. All-electric is the default choice anyway.
  • A heat-pump hot water system scores well in the NCC 2022 whole-of-home energy budget.
  • Reverse-cycle split or ducted systems: check the energy rating, zoning and the refrigerant licence (ARCtick) of the installer.
  • Solar PV: use a Solar Accreditation Australia (formerly CEC) accredited installer if you want rebates. Check Solar Victoria.
Electrical: switchboard, lighting, smoke alarms

Standards: AS/NZS 3000 (wiring rules), AS 3786 (smoke alarms).

  • RCD (safety switch) protection on all final sub-circuits.
  • Hardwired, interconnected smoke alarms in the locations the NCC requires.
  • Plan the points before rough-in: walk every room in My Build and note GPOs, switches, data and TV.
  • Think ahead: an EV charger circuit, induction cooktop capacity, battery-ready switchboard.
Plumbing fixtures & tapware
  • Only buy WaterMark-certified products. Plumbers can refuse to install anything else, and it may not pass inspection.
  • Check WELS water ratings (star rating and litres per minute).
  • Hot water to bathrooms must be delivered at a maximum of 50°C (tempering or thermostatic valve per AS/NZS 3500.4).

Fit-out

Wet areas: waterproofing & tiles

Standard: AS 3740 (waterproofing of domestic wet areas).

  • Get a waterproofing certificate for every wet area. The building surveyor needs it.
  • Slip resistance: use P3 or better for wet-area floors and P4–P5 for outdoor areas (AS 4586).
  • Order about 10% extra tiles from one batch (15% for diagonal or herringbone). My Build's room calculator does this for you.
Kitchen, benchtops & joinery
  • Engineered stone is banned in Australia from 1 July 2024. Consider porcelain, sintered stone, natural stone, laminate or timber.
  • Board: E0 / E1 low-formaldehyde, moisture-resistant under sinks.
  • Sign off shop drawings. Check the appliance cut-out sizes and ventilation clearances first.
  • Appliances: use the Energy Rating label, and check the cooktop's electrical load with your electrician.
Paint, flooring & finishes
  • Low-VOC, washable paints for living areas. Mould-resistant paint for wet areas. Record the colour codes in My Build.
  • Flooring: acclimatise timber before laying. Check the moisture content of the slab.
  • Keep every product's warranty registration and care guide for the handover file.

How to buy

Getting trade pricing as an owner-builder
  • Open trade accounts with your building permit and certificate of consent. Many suppliers give owner-builders a builder's discount.
  • Bundle orders by stage (e.g. all frame hardware together) and ask for a project price.
  • Compare supply-only prices against supply & install quotes using the tender tool.
Deliveries, storage & checking goods
  • Time deliveries to the program. Early deliveries get stolen or damaged; late ones stop trades.
  • Check quantities and condition on arrival and note damage on the docket before you sign it.
  • Store off the ground, covered and secure. Photograph deliveries for insurance.
  • Keep every docket and invoice against its trade package in your budget.
