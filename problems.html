Problems we solve

The paperwork problems that follow tradies home

Running a small trade business means juggling licences, insurance, staff, subbies, certificates and deadlines on top of the work itself. Here's what electricians, plumbers and builders tell us goes wrong, and what Tradies Bureau does about each one.

Running the business

Too much to keep in your head

An electrician needs an REC registration, an A-grade licence and $5m public liability. A plumber needs a licence, warranty cover for every compliance certificate and public liability. Each renews on its own date, and none of them sends you a reminder you can rely on.

"Everything renews on a different date."

Your compliance map lists every licence, registration, policy and ticket that applies to your trade and the work you take on. Each one shows met, due within 30 days, or missing.

"I find out it's lapsed when a builder asks."

A Sunday email lists what needs you, sorted by urgency, and anything critical comes by WhatsApp outside your quiet hours. Text "check" from the ute for a live summary.

"Certificate deadlines slip at the end of a job."

Jobs carry their certificate deadlines: a Certificate of Electrical Safety to the customer within a month (and the shorter steps for prescribed work), and a plumbing compliance certificate within 5 days.

"The once-a-year dates sneak up on me."

LeavePlus returns, TPAR on 28 August, and the WorkCover declaration and early-payment discount come round each year without setting them up again.

"I chase payment claims from memory."

Log a payment claim and the app works out when the schedule and payment are due under Security of Payment rules, then reminds you before those dates pass.

"The paperwork is in emails, the ute and a shoebox."

Photograph a certificate and the expiry date is read off it for you. Connect Google Drive and certificates are saved in your own folder.

"Nobody else can run it if I'm away."

Give a partner or office manager an Admin role. They sign in as themselves and see the same map, so a holiday doesn't stop the business, and a handover or sale later isn't a scramble.

Onboarding employees

Taking on staff without the spreadsheet

Every new starter brings Fair Work paperwork, an award to get right, checks to sight and, for apprentices, supervision rules that Energy Safe Victoria has made mandatory since 1 September 2025.

"Which statements do I have to hand over?"

Fair Work statements are sent and signed off in the app, including the Fixed Term Contract Information Statement, with a reminder when a fixed-term contract is ending.

"Am I paying the right award?"

Record each employee's award and classification. After the 1 July wage review, the digest flags anyone whose pay hasn't been checked since.

"Who has which tickets, and when do they run out?"

Right to work, driver licence, Working with Children, White Card and high-risk work licences are tracked for each person, with their expiry dates.

"Is every apprentice properly supervised?"

Supervision ratios are shown at a glance, so every apprentice has the supervision they need. First-year electrical apprentices need one-to-one direct supervision.

"I don't want staff seeing everything."

Staff get the Employee role and sign in with their own email link. Owner and Admin roles stay with the people who run the business.

Onboarding subcontractors

Subbies checked before they set foot on site

Chasing certificates by text, checking whether they're current, and wondering whether a long-term subbie is really an employee. If their cover lapses mid-job, the risk lands on you.

"Getting subbies to send their paperwork."

Invite a subbie and they upload their own certificates through a link. Subcontract agreements are signed online.

"Is what they sent actually right?"

Uploads wait for you to check them. Accept them, or reject with a reason and the subbie gets a link to send it again.

"What should I make them hold?"

Set the cover your subbies must hold, such as WorkCover and professional indemnity, on top of what their trade legally needs.

"Are they a contractor or an employee?"

A check based on the Fair Work Ombudsman's indicators is saved as a dated record, and possible super liability is flagged. Contractors paid mainly for their labour can be owed super even with an ABN.

"Their insurance ran out halfway through my job."

Subbies are rechecked every night, ABN and GST included. A certificate running out during a job lands in your digest.

"Collecting their end-of-job certificates."

Subbies send their Certificate of Electrical Safety or plumbing compliance certificate to your handover checklist, ready to pass to your client.

Audits and proof

Ready when someone asks to see it

A builder's prequalification, an insurer, a WorkSafe inspector or an auditor wants proof. Usually it means digging through emails for a PDF that might already be out of date.

"Send me your licence and insurance."

Share one live link instead of a pile of PDFs. It reads your records each time it's opened, links registrations to the official register and masks policy numbers.

"How do I know this record is genuine?"

Each record shows how it was checked, from your own upload up to a check against the BPC or Energy Safe Victoria register.

"Who changed this, and when?"

The audit trail records who changed what and when, with CSV exports for audits.

"The auditor needs to look, not touch."

Give them the read-only Auditor role. They sign in as themselves and can't change anything.

"Why isn't this item done?"

Anything you mark "Doesn't apply" keeps its reason, so a gap on the map is explained rather than overlooked.

"Prove the safety paperwork was done."

Incidents and site safety audits are recorded in one place. The 48-hour written notice to WorkSafe is tracked, and an unsent one goes on the critical list.

Builders and head contractors

Awarding work to trades who are covered

A builder carries the risk of every trade on site, and of every rule in the Domestic Building Contracts Act. More for builders.

"I can't tell which bidder is actually licensed."

Put a work package out to quote. Each bidder is checked live against what the job needs, and compliant bidders are listed first.

"Someone talked me into a trade who wasn't covered."

Awarding to a bidder missing a legal requirement asks for a reason, and the reason is kept in the audit log.

"Clients want to see the quotes."

Share a read-only page of the quotes and each bidder's compliance, with prices shown or hidden. Bidders' contact details are never shown.

"Did that claim break the deposit rules?"

Deposits and progress claims are checked against the Act, and home warranty is flagged on domestic contracts over $20,000.

Contract administrators

The paper trail on every job, in one place

Contract administrators keep a job's claims, variations, delays and certificates moving between the builder, the trades and the client, often across several businesses at once. Much of what they chase is already tracked in the app.

Are you a contract administrator? Tell us what you'd want it to do.

"Payment claims and schedules on a deadline."

Security of Payment claims are logged with the dates the schedule and payment fall due, with reminders before either passes.

"Variations agreed on site and never written down."

Variations are signed online and go to the client with a PDF.

"Delays and extensions of time."

A delays and extension of time register sits with each job, beside every mandatory inspection stage and the waterproofing records.

"Chasing certificates for handover."

Trades send their end-of-job certificates to the handover checklist. You accept or reject them, then pass them to the client.

"I work across several builders."

Sign in once and switch between the businesses you work in. Use the read-only Auditor role where you only need to look.
